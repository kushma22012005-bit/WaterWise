THE STORY BEHIND WATERWISE

Every Drop Has a Story.

WaterWise is a water consumption analysis and forecasting project that transforms historical records into useful insights. By exploring consumption patterns and environmental conditions, it helps users understand water usage and make more informed planning decisions.

W

What is WaterWise?

WaterWise combines data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, visualization, machine learning and model evaluation in one web application. Users can explore historical consumption statistics, view trends and submit selected conditions to obtain a model-based consumption estimate.

The Problem We Address

Water usage can change with time, temperature, rainfall, sunshine and other conditions. Looking at raw records alone can make it difficult to identify patterns or estimate possible demand. WaterWise makes these records easier to explore through visual summaries and a forecasting interface. Its predictions are estimates based on available data, not guaranteed future values.

How WaterWise Works

From historical records to a forecast, the application follows a data-driven workflow.

01

Data Collection

Load the historical dataset containing consumption, date, time and environmental information.

02

Data Preprocessing

Prepare the data, check its quality and create the features needed by the model.

03

Exploratory Analysis

Inspect consumption patterns and use visualizations to better understand the dataset.

04

Model Training

Train the forecasting model using the historical data and the features defined by the project.

05

Performance Evaluation

Review metrics such as MAE, RMSE and R² when available to understand prediction quality.

06

Forecast and Explore

Submit input conditions and explore consumption summaries and trends through the website.

Scope and Limitations

WaterWise is an educational data science project designed to explore water consumption and forecasting. Results depend on dataset quality, the features used by the model and how well historical observations represent future conditions. The application should be used to explore patterns and estimates, not as a substitute for actual water measurements or an operational water-management system.
